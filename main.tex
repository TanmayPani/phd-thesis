\abstract{
%
Most of all stable matter is made of protons and neutrons in the atomic nuclei.
%
Protons and neutrons, or hadrons are also composite objects made of quarks and gluons, or partons which are as far as we know, fundamental particles of the strong nuclear force or just the strong force.
%
The field theory Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD) formalizes properties of the strong force that makes it impossible for partons to escape hadrons under ``normal'' circumstances.
%
However, when matter is heated up to extremely high temperatures  or compressed to extremely  high pressure, the same property of QCD will lead to the hadronic matter undergoing a phase transition into a partonic state consisting of free quarks and gluons outside the hadronic volume, called Quark-Gluon Plasma (QGP). 
%

QGP is created in the laboratory by collidiing heavy-nuclei like $\rm ^{96}_{40}Zr$, $\rm ^{96}_{44}Ru$, $\rm ^{197}_{79}Au$, etc. at near light fields. 
%
In the very early stages of these collisions, before QGP formation, partons undergo hard scattering and produce highly energetic outgoing partons. 
%
In vaccum, these hard scattered partons would undergo successive fragmentations and eventually evolve into spatially dense ensembles of hadrons called jets
%
However in heavy ion collisions, these partons go through the QGP, undergoing extra radiative and collisional energy losses. 
%
These extra interactions change  the spatial compositions of hadrons inside jets, compared to the vaccum case. 
%
This phenomenon is called jet quenching, a commonly used probe of QGP, with the vaccum case approximated by eqivalent proton-proton (\(p+p\)) collisions. 

Two jet analyses are presented in this thesis.
%
The first,  jet-hadron correlations in Au+Au collisions at \sNN{200}{GeV}, explores the dependence of jet quenching on length of the path travelled in QGP, by calculating changes in average azimuthal distribution of final state hadrons relative to axes of differently oriented jets. 

The second analyses characterizes angular spread of the jet's energy through observables called generalized angularities. 
%
The analysis begins with \(p+p\) collisions at \sPP{200}{GeV} showing distributions of various generalized angularities, which will eventually become baselines for heavy-ion measurements. 
%
Nest, SoftDrop grooming applied on these jets separates the effects of jets early and late evolution. 
%
We close with inital studies for ongoing measurement of heavy-ion measurements of generalized angularities.

Along the way, various computational techniques studied for these analyses are also discussed.
}
