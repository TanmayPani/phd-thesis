\chapter{Summary}
\label{ch:summary}
The jet-hadron correlations relative to the event plane of Ch.~\ref{ch:jhcorr} have been published by the STAR Collaboration in Phys.\ Rev.\ C~\textbf{109}, 044909 (2024)~\cite{STAR:2023hye}.
%
The generalized angularities of jets in \AuAu collisions (Sec.~\ref{sec:AuAuAngularities}) were shown as STAR preliminary results at Quark Matter 2023~\cite{Pani:2024mgy}.
%
Earlier iterations of the \pp angularities of Sec.~\ref{sec:ppResults} were presented orally at Hard Probes 2023~\cite{Pani_2024} and as posters at Hard Probes 2024 and 2026, and some of the recommendations of this work for unbinned unfolding are featured in a community white paper~\cite{Canelli_2026}.

This thesis used jets to study QCD in vacuum and in the quark-gluon plasma at RHIC, with two questions in mind: does jet quenching depend on the path length through the medium, and how is the energy of a jet distributed in angle?
%
Because soft gluon radiation is ordered in angle (App.~\ref{app:theory}), the second question is a question about the history of the parton shower.

\paragraph{Path-length dependence of jet quenching.}
Ch.~\ref{ch:jhcorr} measured the distributions of charged hadrons around \pttrigrange{15}{20} and \pttrigrange{20}{40} jets in 20--50\% central \AuAu collisions at \sNN{200}{GeV}, separately for jets in-, mid- and out-of-plane, with the flow-modulated background removed by the reaction plane fit.
%
Within the uncertainties, neither the associated yields nor the widths of the jet peaks show a dependence on the event plane.
%
The ratios of the associated yields between the orientations do not deviate significantly from unity, with indications of potential modifications only in the inclusive \ptassocrange{1.0}{10} bin of the \pttrigrange{20}{40} jets.
%
The finite event-plane resolution, \(R_2 = 0.56\), dilutes any real dependence, and JEWEL predicts one well below the uncertainties, consistent with jet-by-jet fluctuations dominating over path-length effects.

\paragraph{Jet substructure in \AuAu collisions.}
The girth, \(p_\mathrm{T}^\mathrm{D}\) and LeSub of hard-core jets (Sec.~\ref{sec:AuAuAngularities}), the first heavy-ion measurement corrected with unbinned, machine-learning based unfolding, are consistent between 0--20\% and 40--80\% central collisions within the systematic uncertainties.

\paragraph{Inclusive and groomed angularities in \pp collisions.}
The \pp measurement of Sec.~\ref{sec:ppResults} provides the inclusive and SoftDrop-groomed angularities \(\lambda^{\kappa=1}_{\beta}\), \(\beta = 0.5\), 1 and 2, of inclusive jets at \sPP{200}{GeV}, validated by closure tests and by reproducing four previously published STAR observables from the same unfolded jets.
%
Its main physics message comes from combining two views of the same jets.
%
The distributions show that PYTHIA\mbox{-}8 and HERWIG\mbox{-}7 predict jets narrower than measured, by up to 20--40\% in the tails, while PYTHIA\mbox{-}6 agrees within uncertainties in most bins.
%
The mean angularities as functions of \(R_\mathrm{g}\), an observable that did not have to be chosen before the unfolding, locate the origin of this difference: at every splitting angle PYTHIA\mbox{-}8 and HERWIG\mbox{-}7 fall 10--15\% below the data, so they produce too little radiation at a given angle rather than a wrong distribution of angles.

\paragraph{Synthesis.}
Both heavy-ion measurements select jets with a high-tower trigger and hard-core constituents, which favors hard-fragmented, narrow jets that interact least with the medium.
%
This survivor bias is the likely common reason why neither the expected path-length dependence nor a centrality dependence of the substructure is observed.
%
The \pp measurement avoids this selection and provides both the baseline and a validated unbinned unfolding pipeline for the next generation of heavy-ion measurements.

\section{Outlook}
The natural next step is to measure the inclusive and groomed angularities of jets in \AuAu collisions with the constituent selection, grooming and unfolding of the \pp analysis.
%
There, the \(R_\mathrm{g}\) profiles can separate a medium-induced change of the radiation at a given splitting angle from a change of the splitting angles themselves, a distinction one-dimensional distributions cannot make.
%
The unfolding itself can be made more robust by methods that remove the need for iterations~\cite{Ore:2026qgp}, which would avoid the growth of statistical fluctuations over the iterations seen in this work, and by profiling the detector uncertainties as nuisance parameters~\cite{Zhu:2024drd}.

On the jet-hadron side, selecting events on the ellipticity of their initial state through the \(q_n\) flow vector would test whether fluctuations of the initial geometry wash out the event-plane dependence.
%
The classifiers of App.~\ref{app:ML} suggest that medium modification can itself be quantified as a discrimination problem.
%
Finally, the \pp measurement will be extended with \(\lambda_0^2\) and the remaining angularities for the final STAR publication.
